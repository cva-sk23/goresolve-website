What is an OTS Request Letter?

An OTS (One Time Settlement) request letter is a formal written request to your bank asking them to accept a lump sum payment — lower than the total outstanding — as full and final settlement of your loan. Once accepted, the loan is closed and no further recovery action can be taken.

Key fact: Banks accept OTS for loans that have become NPA (Non-Performing Assets). They prefer recovering some money over court battles that take years. Most banks accept 40–60% of principal outstanding as OTS.

Free Sample OTS Request Letter Format

To,
The Branch Manager / Loan Recovery Department
[Bank Name], [Branch Name]

Subject: Request for One Time Settlement of Loan Account No. [XXXXXXXX]

Respected Sir/Madam,

I, [Your Full Name], holder of loan account number [XXXXXXXX] with your bank, am writing to request a One Time Settlement of my outstanding loan.

Due to [mention genuine reason — job loss / medical emergency / business loss / pay cut], I am unable to repay the full outstanding amount of ₹[total outstanding]. I sincerely wish to resolve this matter and close my loan account.

I propose to pay ₹[your offer amount] as a full and final settlement of the entire outstanding dues including principal, interest, and penalties. I request you to kindly consider this offer and provide me a written OTS sanction letter at the earliest.

I assure you that upon receipt of your approval, I will arrange the payment within [X] days.

Thanking you,
[Your Full Name]
[Mobile Number]
[Date]

What to Include in Your OTS Letter — Checklist

  • Your full name and loan account number
  • The total outstanding amount as per bank records
  • Your offer amount (realistic — 40–60% of principal)
  • Genuine reason for hardship — documented if possible
  • Timeline for payment after approval
  • Request for written OTS sanction letter
  • Request that CIBIL be updated to "Closed" not "Settled" if possible

What Happens After You Submit the OTS Letter?

1

Bank reviews your request (7–30 days)

The bank's recovery or NPA department reviews your offer. They may counter-offer with a higher amount. Be prepared to negotiate — rarely do banks accept the first offer.

2

Negotiation and final agreement

Agree on the final amount. Get it in writing — a formal OTS sanction letter from the bank on their letterhead. Do not pay anything without this letter.

3

Make payment and get NOC

Pay the agreed amount within the stipulated time. After payment, the bank must issue a No Objection Certificate (NOC) and update your CIBIL record.

Never pay without written OTS sanction: Verbal agreements mean nothing. Always insist on a written OTS sanction letter before paying even a rupee. Banks have been known to accept payment and still pursue recovery.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will OTS affect my CIBIL score?

Yes. A settled loan is marked "Settled" in CIBIL — not "Closed." This impacts future loan eligibility for 7 years. However, it is far better than a continued default or court judgement.

Can I apply for OTS on multiple loans?

Yes. Each loan must have a separate OTS request. Banks evaluate each loan account independently.

What if the bank rejects my OTS?

You can revise your offer upward and reapply. Also consider approaching a professional like GOresolve to negotiate on your behalf — banks often respond differently to structured proposals.
