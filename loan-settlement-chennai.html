What Is Loan Settlement (OTS) and How Does It Work?

Loan settlement — formally called a One-Time Settlement (OTS) — is an arrangement where a bank or NBFC agrees to close your loan account by accepting a lump-sum payment that is less than the total outstanding dues. The bank writes off the remaining balance, and you receive a No Dues Certificate (NDC).

Banks in Chennai — including Indian Bank, IOB, Canara Bank, SBI, and private lenders — consider OTS when an account has gone into NPA (Non-Performing Asset) status, typically after 90 days of non-payment. The bank would rather recover a portion now than chase the full amount through prolonged legal proceedings.

✅ When Banks Agree to Settle

  • Account is 90+ days past due (NPA or pre-NPA)
  • Borrower demonstrates genuine financial hardship
  • The lump sum offered is better than what they'd recover in court
  • The loan is unsecured (personal loan, credit card) — easier to settle than secured loans
  • The borrower submits a formal, documented settlement request

Loan Settlement Options in Chennai — Which One Applies to You?

1. Personal Loan Settlement

Personal loans are unsecured, which makes them the most settleable. Banks typically accept 40–60% of the principal outstanding. Interest waivers of 50–100% are common for accounts over 180 days overdue. See our detailed guide: Personal Loan Settlement Chennai →

2. Credit Card Settlement

Credit card dues accumulate fast due to compounding interest. Banks often waive a large portion of interest and penalties during settlement. A ₹3 lakh outstanding can sometimes settle for ₹1–1.5 lakh. See: Credit Card Settlement Chennai →

3. Business / MSME Loan Settlement

For business loans, the bank evaluates the enterprise's viability. If the business is no longer operating, banks may accept lower settlements. MSME owners in Chennai, Coimbatore, and Tiruppur have specific RBI schemes available.

4. Home Loan Settlement

Home loans are secured — meaning the bank holds the property as collateral. Full settlement before auction is still possible and often preferable for both parties. The timeline and amounts differ significantly from unsecured loans.

⚠️ What Banks Will Not Tell You

  • The first offer is rarely their best — always negotiate
  • Banks have internal OTS approval committees — decisions aren't made by the agent you speak to
  • A well-written Hardship Letter dramatically improves approval chances
  • You can request a settlement even if a legal notice has already been sent

Step-by-Step: How to Apply for Loan Settlement in Chennai

1

Get your current outstanding statement

Request a detailed account statement from your bank showing the principal, interest, and penalty components. You need these numbers to negotiate intelligently.

2

Write a formal OTS request letter

Address it to the Branch Manager or Recovery Department. State your financial hardship clearly, your inability to pay the full amount, and propose a specific settlement figure. The tone should be factual and respectful — not emotional.

3

Submit with supporting documents

Include income proof (salary slips or ITR), bank statements showing financial stress, and any medical or job-loss documentation if applicable. A well-documented request is approved faster.

4

Negotiate the counter-offer

The bank will counter. Do not accept the first offer. There is typically room for a second or third negotiation round. Stay firm on a number you can actually pay — committing to a settlement you cannot complete is worse than not settling.

5

Get everything in writing before paying

Do not pay a single rupee until you have the settlement offer letter from the bank in writing, signed by an authorized officer. This letter protects you legally.

6

Pay and collect your No Dues Certificate

After payment, the bank issues an NDC (No Dues Certificate) and updates the account as "Settled" in CIBIL. Keep the NDC permanently — it is your proof that the account is closed.

Loan Settlement and CIBIL Score — What Actually Happens

This is the most misunderstood part of settlement. Here is the truth:

  • After settlement, your CIBIL report shows the account status as "Settled" — not "Closed"
  • "Settled" is negative — it signals to future lenders that you did not repay in full
  • However, "Settled" is significantly better than "Written Off" or active NPA status
  • Your CIBIL score will take a temporary hit but can recover over 12–24 months

Loan Settlement vs Foreclosure — Which Is Better?

Foreclosure means paying off the full outstanding amount early. Settlement means paying a reduced amount. For borrowers who can pay the full amount, foreclosure is always better — it leaves a clean "Closed" status on CIBIL. For borrowers in genuine financial distress, settlement is the practical option. Frequently Asked Questions

Can I settle a loan that is already in court?

Yes. Even if the bank has filed a legal case, settlement is possible and often encouraged by courts as an alternative. You can approach the bank directly or through the court-appointed mediator.

Do I need a lawyer for loan settlement?

Not necessarily. A certified debt recovery professional who understands the bank's internal process can be more effective than a lawyer for negotiation. Lawyers are useful if the case is already in DRT (Debt Recovery Tribunal).

Can banks refuse to settle?

Yes — banks are not legally obligated to offer OTS. However, for accounts in NPA, it is almost always in the bank's financial interest to settle rather than pursue legal recovery.

What happens if I cannot pay the agreed settlement amount?

The settlement agreement becomes void and the bank can resume recovery action. This is why it is critical to commit only to a figure you can definitively pay within the agreed timeline.
